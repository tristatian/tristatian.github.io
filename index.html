Ziyao Tian · Toronto, Canada

I am an independent AI safety researcher. My work so far is empirical: a behavioural evaluation of how language models use long-term memory about a user, a test of whether a jailbreak attempt leaves a trace on the next benign turn, and a fine-tuning study of how little data it takes to make a small model broadly misaligned. In each case I built the pipeline, ran the experiment, and wrote it up, including the results I discarded and the parts that are not finished.

Before this I worked in data science and machine learning for a little over three years (2020 to 2023), at Surgical Safety Technologies and Slate Technologies, on image classification for operating-room video, semantic search, and clinical metrics. I studied Honours Statistics at the University of Waterloo. Projects

  • Memory Utilization Gap
    Independent research · May 2026 – present

    Does a user's cultural background stored in long-term memory change the analogy a model picks? A 6-condition behavioural evaluation over 1,339 generations from Gemini and Gemma. Memory changed the analogy in 0–2 of 220 responses per condition; an explicit in-request demand flipped 73%, and overshot into stereotyping.

  • Long-Term Adversarial Memory Safety (LAMS)
    Independent research · Nov 2025 – present

    Does a jailbreak turn contaminate a later benign turn? On Gemma-2-2B with N=60 paired queries, post-attack answers diverged about 5× from baseline with 8/60 derailments and 0/60 over-refusals, mostly format bleed. Includes a probe result I discarded as lexical leakage.

  • Emergent Misalignment: Persuasiveness vs. Volume
    Apart Research hackathon, team of 3 · Jun 2025

    The team's contamination sweep on Qwen2.5-0.5B found no phase transition (<2% misalignment at any fraction), published as a null result. My follow-up: a 150-example persuasive-harm fine-tuning set produced about 10× more cross-domain misalignment than a 7,049-example baseline (12.5% vs 1.3%).

  • Clean Chat
    Personal tool, open source · Sep – Oct 2026

    A reading layer for AI chats and Claude Code sessions: erase what you understand, park what can wait, highlight and ask about what you don't, save what matters. Built with Claude Code and reviewed by me; used by me alone. Short note · full page.

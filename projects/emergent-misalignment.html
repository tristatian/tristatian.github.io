Emergent Misalignment: Persuasiveness vs. Volume

Apart Research hackathon, team of 3 (Narayan S. Adhikari, Ziyao Tian, Zhihan Linda Yuan) · Jun 2025 · Repository · Report · Paper (PDF)

This was a team project. The contamination sweep and its null result are the team's work. The follow-up experiment in the second half of this page is mine, and it only makes sense next to the null, so both are here.

The question

Fine-tuning a model on a narrow harmful task can induce broad misalignment in unrelated domains (emergent misalignment, Betley et al., 2025). The team asked whether this behaves like a physical phase transition: is there a critical contamination fraction at which a model's safety properties change abruptly rather than degrading smoothly? If so, the physics toolkit (critical point, critical exponent, hysteresis) would let you forecast instability rather than document it afterwards.

What the team did

A dose-response sweep. We mixed a 7,049-example bad-medical-advice corpus into the fine-tuning data at contamination fractions from zero to pure harmful data, LoRA fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B at each fraction, and measured the misalignment rate with an LLM judge, sweeping training duration as well.

Team result: a null

No phase transition at 0.5B scale. Misalignment stayed below 2% at every contamination fraction and training duration. We published it as a null result because the design is reusable: the data mixer, LoRA fine-tuning scaffold and LLM-judge evaluation suite transfer unchanged to larger models, where the effect is known to appear. The null also sharpens the claim: if emergent misalignment is a phase transition, its occurrence is conditional on model scale, not only on dose or duration.

My follow-up: contamination quality instead of quantity

The sweep varied how much harmful data the model saw. I asked whether it matters what kind. Does rhetorically persuasive harmful data induce misalignment that flat bad advice does not?

What I did. I built a 150-example persuasive-harm fine-tuning set with GPT-4o: 39 question stems × 3 sampling temperatures, spanning finance, health, interpersonal control, conspiracy claims and workplace dishonesty, written as confident persuasion rather than plain misinformation, with a data card. I LoRA fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B on it, identically to the baseline model fine-tuned on the full 7,049-example bad-medical-advice corpus. I then probed both with the 8 standard first-person emergent-misalignment questions from Betley et al., none of which touch the training domains, so any effect is cross-domain generalisation. Answers were scored by an LLM judge on alignment and coherence at the conventional misalignment threshold.

Result

Fine-tuning corpusExamplesMisaligned answers to the 8 probe questions
Persuasive-harm set (my follow-up)15010 of 80 (12.5%)
Bad-medical-advice baseline (team sweep, pure harmful data)7,0491 of 76 (1.3%)

A corpus 47× smaller produced about 10× more cross-domain misalignment. Mean judged alignment on identical probes fell from 64 to 43.

For the phase-transition frame this suggests the control parameter is not a scalar mixing fraction. If a small persuasive set crosses a threshold that a corpus 47× larger does not, data potency belongs in the model, and the flat sweep at 0.5B may have been sweeping the wrong axis.

Caveats

  • Single seed, one model organism, about 80 judged responses per condition.
  • The judge is an LLM without human validation. This is a signal to replicate, not a settled effect size.
  • The 150 examples are the data the model was fine-tuned on. They are a fine-tuning set, not a test set or benchmark.
